If you're having an urge

  1. Move. Get up and go to a different room, or step outside. Changing where you are breaks the pattern.
  2. Put the phone down. Leave it in another room, or hand it to someone you trust.
  3. Reach out. Text or call your sponsor, a friend from group, or anyone safe. You don't have to explain much. "Having a rough moment" is enough.
  4. Check in with yourself. Are you hungry, angry, lonely, or tired? Take care of that one thing first.
  5. Ride it out. Start the timer above. Notice the urge without acting on it. It will rise, peak, and fade.
  6. Write it down after. What set it off, and what helped? The coping skills log keeps this private.

If you slipped, you haven't lost your progress. Be honest with yourself and the people who support you, and start again today. The 90-day tracker lets you mark a slip and keep going.

If you just found out, and you're hurting

  1. Breathe first. Use the timer above. Shock can make your body feel out of control. That's a normal reaction to a real injury.
  2. You don't have to decide anything tonight. Not about the relationship, and not about what comes next. Big decisions can wait until you feel steadier.
  3. Call someone safe. A close friend, a family member, or a counselor. S-Anon also offers phone support with people who've been through this.
  4. Look after your body. Drink water, eat something small, and try to rest. When you're ready, it's okay to see a doctor about your own health.
  5. Step away from searching. Checking phones and digging for details late at night usually makes the pain worse, not clearer. You can ask your questions later, in a safer setting.
  6. It isn't your fault. Another person's choices are theirs. Your healing matters on its own, whatever happens with the relationship.

  • 3 things you can hear
  • 2 things you can smell
